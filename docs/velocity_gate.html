Velocity Gate

1. What it is

The velocity gate is a safety filter between move_base and the robot. TEB decides where to go. The gate only decides how fast that command may be executed, given what the lidar sees right now.

move_base (TEB) ──/cmd_vel_nav──▶ velocity_gate ──/cmd_vel──▶ Unity robot
                                      ▲
                     /scan ───────────┤   (+ TF odom ← laser_link)
SituationGate reaction
Static obstacle ahead in the pathSlow in proportion to the gap, stop 0.30 m from the nose, then HOLD
Head-on moving obstacle on a collision course, within 1.5 mHOLD immediately: v = 0 and w = 0, whatever TEB asks
Side obstacle that keeping TEB's speed avoidsnothing, keep going
Side obstacle that would hit us; passing at 0.32 m/s avoids itPASS at 0.32 m/s, even if TEB slowed down
Side obstacle we can't pass; stopping lets it cross in frontHOLD crossing, only once waiting longer would be unsafe
Side obstacle where stopping would leave us in its pathESCAPE: pass anyway (also ends a HOLD)

The distinction: what kind of motion (head-on vs side), and for side obstacles which robot plan is predicted to be safe. Head-on means yield. Side means continue, then pass, then stop, in that order. Stop is chosen only when it really keeps the robot out of the path.

  • It only publishes while move_base is commanding (a /cmd_vel_nav message within 0.3 s), plus one zero Twist when move_base goes idle, so the robot is never left driving. Docking and lift scripts write /cmd_vel directly and are never gated.
  • The scan callback and the timer share the logic under a lock. A timer error is logged and answered with a zero Twist; the timer keeps running.
  • During HOLD the output is a fully zero Twist. TEB keeps planning swerves, but none of them reach the robot, so it no longer stops and weaves.
  • The lidar is a 180° arc 0.33 m ahead of drive_center. Moving obstacles that go out of view beside the robot are carried on their last velocity (coasting).

3. Moving-obstacle detection: FreeSpaceDetector

A free-space violation test: if 0.4 s ago the laser saw through a spot that now returns a hit, something moved there.

  1. Keep 1.5 s of scans, each with the laser pose in odom at its stamp (from TF).
  2. Pick a reference scan at least moving_ref_age = 0.4 s old.
  3. For each current return: convert it to an odom point, and look at it from the reference laser pose (angle and distance ρ).
  4. Take the smallest free distance among the reference ray at that angle and its two neighbours (inf = free to range_max).
  5. If that free distance is greater than ρ + moving_tol (0.12 m), the return is flagged moving.
CaseReference ray at that spotFlagged?
Static wall, robot driving or turninghits the same spot (odom frame cancels own motion)no
Obstacle coming toward the robotpassed through to its old, farther positionyes
Obstacle crossing the pathpassed through to the wall behindyes
Obstacle moving awayspot was hidden behind itno
Static surface newly revealedspot was hiddenno

The minimum over three neighbouring rays stops points beside a static edge from being flagged. Sensitivity is about 0.12 m / 0.4 s ≈ 0.3 m/s. If TF odom ← laser_link is missing for a scan, tracks keep coasting on a dead-reckoned pose, so one missed lookup doesn't abandon a pass. After 1 s without a pose (pose_timeout) tracking is off and the gate falls back to its distance-only rules.

4. Tracking moving obstacles

  • gap = min (px − nose_x) over corridor points (|py| ≤ half_w), moving or not. This drives the static rules.
  • Clusters: consecutive returns closer than 0.25 m form a segment. A segment with ≥ 2 moving returns is a moving obstacle; its visible bounding box is kept (within 5 m ahead, 3.5 m to the side).
  • Tracks in odom, matched to the nearest predicted track (0.6 m + its size). Velocity is the median of scan-to-scan steps over 0.6 s, so a one-off jump when another face comes into view doesn't corrupt it.
  • A box cut off at the edge of the lidar's view only updates the sideways position.
  • Coasting: a track that should be visible but isn't seen for 0.5 s is dropped. Out of view beside or behind the laser, it coasts at its last velocity for up to 4 s, so a pass isn't abandoned halfway.
  • Used for decisions after 0.25 s of history and ≥ 0.15 m/s. Classified by its own motion in the robot frame: |uy| ≥ |ux| → SIDE; ux < 0 → HEAD-ON; moving away → ignored.

5. Per-command decision (20 Hz)

5.1 Front speed target

d_slow = d_stop + max(v, 0.05) · horizon_s
target = clamp((gap − d_stop) / (d_slow − d_stop), 0, 1)
slow zone ends at scale sent

Static obstacle only. Braking applies immediately; speeding up ramps at 0.8 per second. A scan older than 0.5 s gives target 0 (forward blocked, no HOLD).

5.2 Moving obstacles: predict three plans

PlanRobot travel s(t), straight, over 7 s
continuev · t (TEB's speed)
pass0.32 · front_target(0.32) · t (no passing into a static obstacle)
stopmin(v_now · t, b), with b = 0.3·v_now + v_now²/(2·0.5)

The clearance of a plan is the smallest gap between the footprint and the moving obstacle box over the horizon (negative = overlap depth). A plan is safe when clearance > 0.1 m + obstacle speed · 0.3 s.

only for tracks where "continue" is not safe:

HEAD-ON:  within 1.5 m of the nose                      → HOLD "moving"
          further away                                  → nothing yet

SIDE:     0. already passing: until it is behind the fork tips
             (or has crossed), keep PASS while clearance > 0
          1. pass safe                                       → PASS
          2. continue 0.5 s more, then stop, still safe      → nothing yet
          3. stop safe                                       → HOLD "crossing"
          4. none safe: stop clearance > pass clearance     → HOLD "crossing"
                        otherwise (ties too)                 → ESCAPE
  • Pass is absolute while TEB commands forward (v > 0): output max(TEB v, 0.32), |w| ≤ 0.2. TEB slowing for the obstacle can't stall the robot in its path, but a zero, rotate-in-place or reverse command is never turned into a pass.
  • Commitment is physical: once passing, it keeps going until the obstacle is behind the fork tips (x = −0.54) by the margin, or has crossed and moves away. It doesn't stop as soon as TEB's speed is merely predicted to be enough.
  • Stop must be earned: a side HOLD needs a stop plan that keeps clearance; otherwise ESCAPE, which also ends a running HOLD.
  • No premature stop: it yields only when stopping 0.5 s later would no longer be safe.
  • Side obstacles never trigger the head-on rule, even when their edge enters the corridor during a pass.

5.3 State machine

ReasonConditionNote
movinghead-on track on a collision course within 1.5 many command, including rotate-in-place

Clear means all of: the scan is fresh; gap > 0.45 m (d_stop + clear_margin); no moving-obstacle hold reason (while holding, "continue" itself must be safe). The 1 s timer restarts whenever any of these fails. ESCAPE leaves HOLD immediately.

Statev_outw_out
HOLD00

Why GIVEUP: during a hold move_base sees no progress. After gate_hold_timeout (20 s) the gate releases rotation, so move_base's nav_patience (30 s) and oscillation_timeout (60 s) never abort the goal. A launch-contract test enforces this ordering.

Printed while output is reduced (scale < 0.99) and still moving forward. S = the fraction of TEB's speed sent. Not printed during HOLD, during GIVEUP with forward blocked, or with a stale scan.

8. scan_clearing (companion node)

costmap_2d erases a cell only when a ray passes through it. That leaves trails between the 1° rays (more than one cell apart beyond about 3 m) and in the blind rear half. /scan_clearing is a 360° scan used with marking: false, so it can only erase:

  • Front: 4× denser rays. Each inserted ray reaches only as far as the nearer neighbour; a nan neighbour means clear nothing.
  • Rear blind arc: rays through the static /map, stopping 0.25 m before occupied, unknown or off-map cells, capped at 4 m. Racks stay; trails go.
[scan_clearing] no map pose, blind arc not cleared: …WARN every 10 s; no AMCL map → odom yet. Front densification still works
[scan_clearing] rotated map origin not supported; …map yaml has a non-zero origin yaw

Trade-off: objects not on the static map are forgotten once beside or behind the robot. If AMCL drift erases rack marks, raise ~map_margin.
